<p>The first time I baked brownies over a campfire, I used a boxed mix that called for eggs and oil—and I forgot the eggs. The result was a crumbly, sad slab that tasted like cocoa-flavored sand. That's when I learned that GFCF brownies need a little more structure, especially when you're working with a Dutch oven and a pile of coals. But with the right ratios and a few tricks, you can have fudgy, rich brownies that satisfy even the most devoted chocolate lover—no gluten, no dairy, no problem.</p>

<h2>Why GFCF Brownies Work at Camp</h2>
<p>Gluten-free flours like almond flour bring moisture and fat, which means your brownies stay tender instead of turning into a brick. Dairy-free fats like coconut oil solidify at cooler temperatures, so they hold up better when packed in a cooler. And because you're controlling the ingredients, you avoid the hidden dairy powder that sneaks into many commercial baking mixes.</p>
<p>The key is to treat the batter gently. Overmixing develops the little gluten that might be present in your flour blend, and it also knocks air out of the eggs, giving you a dense, tough result. Stir until the streaks disappear, then stop.</p>

<h2>Choosing Your Baking Vessel</h2>
<p>At home, an 8x8-inch metal pan works perfectly. At camp, a 10-inch cast iron Dutch oven is ideal because it distributes heat evenly and holds temperature well. If you're using a camp stove, a heavy-bottomed pot with a tight-fitting lid can work, but you'll need to keep the heat low and check often.</p>
<p><strong>Common mistake:</strong> putting the Dutch oven directly on high flames. That scorches the bottom and leaves the middle raw. Instead, use briquettes or a campfire grate with coals. Aim for about 8 briquettes underneath and 14 on top for a 350°F (175°C) oven. Rotate the oven a quarter turn every 10 minutes to avoid hot spots.</p>

<h2>Campfire Timing and Temperature</h2>
<p>Coals from a wood fire can be unpredictable. Start checking the brownies at 20 minutes. Insert a toothpick or a thin knife into the center—it should come out with a few moist crumbs, not wet batter. If the top is browning too fast, move some coals off the lid. If it's pale and jiggly, add a few more.</p>
<p>Cold weather and wind can extend baking time by 10 minutes or more. Keep a windbreak (a large rock or a cooler) near the oven. And remember: a Dutch oven holds heat, so pull it off the heat source a minute or two before you think it's done. Carryover cooking will finish the job.</p>

<h2>GFCF Brownies for Kids and Picky Eaters</h2>
<p>These brownies have a deep chocolate flavor and a soft, fudgy texture that kids usually love. If you're camping with little ones, let them help measure the dry ingredients and stir—it's a great way to keep them busy while you manage the fire. For a fun twist, press a few marshmallows (check that they're gluten-free) into the top during the last 5 minutes of baking for a s'mores effect.</p>

<h2>Packing and Storage Tips</h2>
<p>Bake these at home before your trip and they'll travel beautifully. Wrap each brownie in foil or parchment paper, then place them in a zip-top bag. They'll keep for three days at room temperature, or up to a week in a cooler. If you're bringing them to a campsite, store them in a sealed container to keep out ants and curious raccoons—always follow wildlife-safe food storage rules.</p>

<h2>Adapting for Rain and Wind</h2>
<p>If a sudden shower hits, don't panic. Move your Dutch oven under a tarp or a canopy. Wind is a bigger enemy than rain—it steals heat from the sides of the oven. Use a windscreen or stack a few flat stones around the base. If the weather turns truly nasty, you can always bake the brownies in a regular oven at home and bring them along.</p>

<h2>Safety Notes</h2>
<p>When using a camp stove or Dutch oven, always set it on a stable, level surface away from tent walls. Never use a stove or lantern inside a tent—carbon monoxide is a silent killer. Keep a bucket of water or a fire extinguisher nearby, and make sure coals are completely out before you leave the site.</p>

<h2>The Takeaway</h2>
<p>GFCF brownies are more than a dietary compromise—they're a chance to slow down, gather around the fire, and enjoy something warm and homemade. With the right techniques, you'll have a dessert that's rich, fudgy, and memorable. And if you forget the eggs? Well, now you know better.</p>

<p>The first time I made french toast sticks for a toddler at camp, I learned a hard lesson: thin sandwich bread turns into a soggy mess the moment it hits the custard. Since then, I've refined my method to produce sturdy, dippable sticks that survive a campfire morning and actually get eaten. Here's the technique that works, along with the why behind each step.</p><h2>Why Sticks Beat Slices for Toddlers</h2><p>Little hands have a hard time gripping a floppy slice of french toast. Sticks are naturally finger-sized, which means less mess and more self-feeding. Plus, they're perfect for dipping—into syrup, yogurt, or even applesauce. When you're camping, that means less cleanup and a happier meal time.</p><h2>The Bread Matters (More Than You Think)</h2><p>Forget the flimsy white sandwich loaf. You need bread with structure. Look for Texas toast, brioche, or a day-old sourdough. These have a denser crumb that soaks up the custard without falling apart. If you only have regular bread, slice it thicker (about 1 inch) and let it sit out overnight to dry. Dry bread absorbs the egg mixture like a sponge, but it holds its shape better.</p><h2>The Custard: Simple but Balanced</h2><p>You don't need a fancy batter. A ratio of 1 large egg to 1/4 cup milk gives you a custard that's rich enough to set but not so heavy it makes the bread mushy. Add a splash of maple syrup for sweetness and a pinch of cinnamon. For babies under one, skip the maple syrup and use a mashed banana instead—it adds natural sweetness and binds the custard nicely.</p><h2>Camp Stove vs. Open Fire</h2><p>A camp stove with a cast-iron skillet is your best bet. It gives you even heat and precise control. If you're cooking over a fire, wait until the flames die down to glowing coals. Place a grill grate over the coals and use a cast-iron skillet—it distributes heat evenly and holds temperature. Keep the heat medium; too high and the outside burns while the inside stays raw.</p><h2>The Soak: A Few Seconds, Not a Bath</h2><p>Here's the mistake I see most often: people let the bread soak too long. You want a quick dip—about 5 seconds per side—just enough to coat the surface. If you soak longer, the bread becomes saturated and falls apart in the pan. For thicker bread, you might need a few extra seconds, but always err on the side of a lighter soak.</p><h2>Cooking in Batches: Keep the First Batch Warm</h2><p>If you're feeding a crowd, cook in batches. Place the finished sticks on a plate and cover loosely with foil. You can also keep them in a warm cooler: line a cooler with a towel, add a few hot water bottles (sealed well), and place the sticks on top. This holds them warm for up to 30 minutes without drying out.</p><h2>Make-Ahead Option for Lazy Mornings</h2><p>Prep these at home and freeze them. Cook the sticks, let them cool completely, then freeze in a single layer on a baking sheet. Once solid, transfer to a zip-top bag. At camp, reheat in a skillet over low heat, turning often, or wrap in foil and warm near the fire. This is a lifesaver when the kids are cranky and you haven't had coffee yet.</p><h2>Dipping Sauces Beyond Syrup</h2><p>Maple syrup is classic, but toddlers love variety. Try vanilla yogurt, applesauce, or a quick berry compote (just simmer frozen berries with a little water and maple syrup). For a no-mess option, put the dip in a silicone muffin cup—it's stable and less likely to spill than a bowl.</p><h2>Safety Notes for Camp Cooking</h2><p>When using a camp stove, always cook outside and never inside a tent. The flame and carbon monoxide are serious risks. Keep a fire extinguisher or a bucket of water nearby. If you're cooking over an open fire, make sure the coals are fully extinguished before leaving the site. And always store any leftover custard or cooked food in a bear-proof container or hung from a tree, depending on local regulations.</p><h2>The Takeaway</h2><p>Toddler french toast sticks are a camping breakfast that doesn't have to be stressful. Choose the right bread, keep the custard light, and don't over-soak. Cook on a camp stove for best results, or use a cast-iron skillet over coals. Make them ahead if you can. And remember: the goal is a happy, full toddler—and a peaceful morning for you.</p>

<p>The first time I made pizza at camp, I was sure it would end up a burnt, doughy mess. But the kids gathered around the skillet like it was a magic trick, and when the cheese started to bubble and the crust turned golden, I knew we'd found a keeper. This isn't just any cheese pizza—it's a method that works with a camp stove or a fire, and it's simple enough that even the littlest campers can help.</p>

<h2>Why This Pizza Works at Camp</h2>
<p>You don't need a fancy outdoor pizza oven. A cast iron skillet does the job beautifully. It holds heat evenly, crisps the bottom, and can go from stove to table without a fuss. The key is to pre-cook the crust for a few minutes before adding toppings. That step ensures the bottom is fully cooked and not doughy, even if the cheese melts slowly.</p>
<p>Another trick: use store-bought dough that's been sitting at room temperature for about 30 minutes. Cold dough is stiff and tears easily. Warm dough stretches like a dream. If you're using a campfire, wait until you have a nice bed of coals, not flames. Direct flames will burn the crust before the cheese melts. Coals give you that steady, even heat you need.</p>

<h2>Gear and Setup</h2>
<p>You'll need a 10-inch cast iron skillet (or a Dutch oven if you're feeding more people), a spatula, a lid or a piece of heavy-duty foil, and a small cutting board for slicing. A rolling pin is nice, but a clean water bottle works in a pinch. Bring a little flour for dusting—it's worth the extra few ounces in your pack.</p>
<p>Set up your cooking area away from tents and overhanging branches. If you're using a camp stove, make sure it's on a stable, level surface. If you're at a fire ring, use a grill grate or a trivet to hold the skillet just above the coals. This gives you more control over the heat.</p>

<h2>The Method, Step by Step</h2>
<p>Start by heating your skillet over medium heat. Add a tablespoon of olive oil and swirl it around. Stretch your dough into a round about the size of the skillet—don't worry about perfect circles; rustic is fine. Lay the dough in the hot skillet and let it cook for two to three minutes. You'll see the bottom start to set and turn golden. This is the secret to a crisp crust.</p>
<p>Flip the crust over so the cooked side is now on top. Spread your sauce, sprinkle the cheese, and add any extras like oregano or a few pepperoni slices if the kids are in the mood. Cover the skillet with a lid or foil. This traps the heat and melts the cheese evenly. Cook for eight to ten minutes, rotating the pan every few minutes to avoid hot spots. Check the bottom—it should be golden and crisp, not burnt. If it's browning too fast, move the skillet to a cooler part of the fire or turn down the stove.</p>

<h3>Common Mistakes to Avoid</h3>
<p>Don't pile on too much sauce or cheese. A thin layer of sauce and a moderate amount of cheese will melt better and keep the crust from getting soggy. Also, resist the urge to peek too often. Every time you lift the lid, you let out heat and extend the cooking time. Trust the process, and check after eight minutes.</p>
<p>If you're cooking with kids, let them help with the fun parts: stretching the dough, sprinkling the cheese, and setting the table. It's a great way to get them excited about dinner and teach them a few camp cooking basics.</p>

<h2>Adapting for Wind, Rain, and Cold</h2>
<p>Wind is the biggest enemy of camp cooking. It can make your stove flame uneven and blow heat away from the skillet. Set up a windbreak with your cooler or a large backpack. If rain is threatening, cook under your tarp or canopy, but keep the stove at least six feet from the tent and never inside. A cold evening? Preheat the skillet longer and keep the lid on. The heat will help warm everyone up.</p>

<h2>Safety Notes</h2>
<p>Always use your camp stove outside and away from tent openings. Never use it inside a tent or enclosed space—carbon monoxide is a silent danger. If you're using a fire, make sure you have water or a shovel nearby to extinguish it completely before leaving the site or going to sleep. And when you're done, let the skillet cool completely before packing it away.</p>

<h2>Takeaway</h2>
<p>This kid-approved cheese pizza is more than a meal—it's an experience. The smell of melting cheese and toasting crust will draw everyone to the table, and the pride of making it together makes it taste even better. With a little practice, you'll have a go-to camp dinner that's easy, satisfying, and guaranteed to please even the pickiest eaters.</p>

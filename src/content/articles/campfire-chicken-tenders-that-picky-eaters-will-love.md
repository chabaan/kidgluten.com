<p>The smell of woodsmoke mingles with the promise of dinner as you unroll a foil packet on the picnic table. Inside, golden chicken tenders steam gently, their edges crisped from the fire. For a picky eater, this is the holy grail: familiar, simple, and ready for a dunk in ketchup or honey mustard.</p>
<figure><img src="https://cdn.midjourney.com/ba5e793c-f527-406d-99f6-dc0897b97123/0_2_640_N.webp" alt="Kids dipping chicken tenders into ketchup and honey mustard at a picnic table" /></figure>


<p>I've spent many nights at camp watching kids push food around their plates. The problem isn't usually the food itself—it's the unfamiliarity. Chicken tenders are a known comfort. The trick is to make them taste like home, not like a campfire experiment. Here's how to pull that off without a kitchen.</p>

<h2>Why Foil Packets Work for Picky Eaters</h2>

<p>Foil packets are the ultimate camping cooking hack for families. They trap steam, keep flavors pure, and make cleanup a breeze. But for picky eaters, the real magic is control. Each packet is its own little meal, so you can customize seasonings without cooking separate dinners.</p>

<p>One child might want just salt and a whisper of garlic. Another might love paprika. By assembling packets individually, everyone gets exactly what they like. That's a win before the first bite.</p>

<h2>The Right Chicken: Fresh vs. Frozen</h2>

<p>Start with chicken tenders—those thin strips that cook quickly and are naturally tender. If you only have chicken breasts, slice them into strips about 1 inch thick. That thickness ensures even cooking over coals.</p>

<p>Frozen tenders can work, but they release extra water that dilutes flavor. If you must use frozen, thaw them fully before packing. I like to prep the packets at home and keep them in the cooler. That way, dinner is just a fire away.</p>

<h2>Seasoning That Appeals to Kids</h2>

<p>Picky eaters often prefer mild flavors. A simple mix of garlic powder, paprika, and salt is usually a safe bet. Paprika adds color without heat. If your child is wary of any spice, start with just salt and a tiny bit of garlic powder. You can always add more after cooking.</p>

<p>Another trick: let the kids choose a dip. Ketchup, honey mustard, barbecue sauce, or even plain yogurt with a little dill. Dipping makes eating fun and gives them a sense of control.</p>

<h2>Building the Perfect Foil Packet</h2>

<p>Use heavy-duty foil, about 12 by 18 inches per packet. Place the tenders in a single layer in the center. Drizzle with a teaspoon of olive oil or a pat of butter. Add a splash of chicken broth or water—just a tablespoon—to create steam and keep the meat juicy.</p>

<p>Fold the long edges together twice, then crimp the short ends tightly. Leave a little air space inside so steam can circulate. This is the difference between moist tenders and rubbery ones.</p>

<h2>Managing the Fire for Even Cooking</h2>

<p>Cook over medium coals, not roaring flames. You want the fire to have burned down to glowing embers. Place the packets on the coals, seam side up. Flip once after about 8 minutes. Total time is usually 15 to 20 minutes, depending on the heat and thickness of the chicken.</p>

<p>If you're using a campfire grate, that works too—just keep the packets a few inches above the coals and turn them frequently. The key is steady, moderate heat. If the fire is too hot, the outside will burn before the inside cooks.</p>

<h2>How to Tell When They're Done</h2>

<p>Safety first: chicken must reach 165°F (74°C) inside. An instant-read thermometer is your best friend. If you don't have one, cut into the thickest tender—the juices should run clear and there should be no pink.</p>

<p>Let the packets rest for three minutes after opening. This lets the juices redistribute, so the meat stays moist. Then serve straight from the packet or transfer to plates.</p>

<h2>Adapting for Wind, Rain, and Cold</h2>

<p>Wind can make coals flare up. If it's gusty, shield the fire with a windbreak or cook on a grate. If rain threatens, have a tarp ready to cover the cooking area. Cold weather means the coals may cool faster, so you might need a few extra minutes of cook time. Just check the temperature before serving.</p>

<p>And if the fire is a no-go due to a burn ban, you can make these same packets on a camp stove with a skillet or a portable grill. The method is the same—just use a low flame and adjust the time.</p>

<h2>Make It a Family Activity</h2>

<p>Let the kids help assemble their own packets. They can sprinkle their own seasonings and choose their dip. This involvement often turns a skeptical eater into a proud chef. Plus, it's a fun way to pass the time while dinner cooks.</p>

<p>One more tip: pack a few extra foil sheets. Kids love making their own mini packets with veggies or fruit. It's messy, but it's memorable.</p>

<h2>The Takeaway</h2>

<p>Campfire chicken tenders are more than just a meal—they're a bridge between the comfort of home and the adventure of the outdoors. With a little prep and the right technique, you can serve a dinner that even the pickiest eater will happily devour. And that, my friend, is the sweetest reward of camping with kids.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<h3>How do I keep chicken tenders from drying out over a campfire?</h3>
<p>The trick is to cook them in a sealed foil packet with a little moisture—like a splash of chicken broth or a pat of butter. Place the packet over medium coals, not roaring flames, and flip once halfway through. Let it rest for 3 minutes before opening so the juices redistribute.</p>
<h3>Can I prep chicken tenders at home for camping?</h3>
<p>Absolutely. Season the tenders and portion them into foil packets at home, then keep them in a cooler with ice packs. At camp, just toss the packets on the fire. This saves time and reduces mess at the campsite.</p>
<h3>What are safe internal temperatures for chicken tenders?</h3>
<p>Chicken must reach an internal temperature of 165°F (74°C) to be safe. Use a small instant-read thermometer to check the thickest tender before serving. If you don't have one, cut into the thickest piece—the juices should run clear and there should be no pink in the center.</p>
